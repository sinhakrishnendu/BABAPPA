\begin{abstract}
Branch-site positive-selection analysis is sensitive to multiple sequence alignment, taxon sampling, saturation, hidden paralogy, and model misspecification. \ProjectName{} (\ProjectExpansion{}) is a research-alpha software and methods framework designed to provide guarded branch-site positive-selection support rather than unsupported empirical discovery calls. The core empirical interface accepts a user-supplied codon multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and matching treefile, treats that MSA as the authoritative homology statement, and scores requested foreground branches or all tree tips. The broader framework combines simulation-supervised explicit branch-site truth, conservative branch-site features, tier-aware neural scoring, calibration and aggregation layers, deployable model packaging, empirical input quality control, out-of-distribution (OOD) applicability gates, optional alignment-sensitivity analysis, and reference-tool comparison scaffolding.

The current implementation, \ProjectVersion{}, completed a conservative explicit branch-truth Apple Silicon/MPS validation at 100,000 simulated families. The validation used four saturation tiers, explicit simulator branch-site truth, the \texttt{conservative\_branch\_site} feature policy, and the production alignment ensemble \texttt{identity}, MAFFT, \texttt{BABAPPAlign}, and MUSCLE. PRANK and T-Coffee are retained only as diagnostic aligners. The 100K run is a conditional pass: raw and intermediate directories were intentionally pruned after completion, but retained summaries, truth audits, model artifacts, calibration outputs, aggregation outputs, controls, and stage markers support the completed validation state. Branch-site neural test AUROC was 0.998605, 0.996962, 0.994527, and 0.989955 for low, moderate, high, and extreme saturation tiers. Branch-level AUROC was 0.999797, 0.999421, 0.998777, and 0.997873. Branch-to-gene AUROC was 0.999697, 0.999616, 0.999508, and 0.999430. These are simulation-supervised validation results only.

The project also includes a validated deployable simulation-trained package for the conservative 100K MPS model family, plus an empirical bridge for direct MSA/tree prediction, input validation, feature extraction, truth-column auditing, applicability/OOD checking, branch-site scoring, empirical reporting, optional codeml/HyPhy-style comparison, and simulation-matched calibration planning. A WRKY empirical pilot demonstrates both abstention and guarded scoring. \texttt{WRKY\_candidate\_01} was correctly treated as an OOD stress test. \texttt{WRKY\_candidate\_02\_close} produced a BABAPPA diagnostic-positive signal on close-taxa WRKY33/AT2G38470 homologs, but codeml and HyPhy references were negative. A 100-replicate feature-level matched-null calibration was completed and showed unusual called-row burden but not unusual maximum gene support. Therefore the correct interpretation is BABAPPA-only, mixed feature-level null support, and inconclusive as an empirical discovery claim. \ProjectName{} is ready as a guarded research-alpha software/methods contribution and for calibrated empirical expansion, but not as a finalized empirical positive-selection discovery engine.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Branch-site positive-selection inference is a central task in molecular evolution. Classical codon models estimate lineage- and site-specific shifts in the nonsynonymous-to-synonymous rate ratio, $\omega=d_N/d_S$, and tools such as PAML/codeml and HyPhy have become standard references for testing episodic diversifying selection \citep{muse1994,goldman1994,yang2007,zhang2005,pond2011_bsrel,smith2015_absrel,murrell2012_meme,murrell2015_busted,pond2020_hyphy}. These methods remain essential. However, they usually condition on a single multiple sequence alignment and a fixed phylogenetic representation. Alignment errors, saturation, recombination, synonymous-rate variation, multinucleotide substitutions, hidden paralogy, and taxon-sampling problems can all distort downstream selection analyses \citep{anisimova2003_recombination,scheffler2006_recombination,jordan2012_alignment,redelings2014_erasing,venkat2018_mnm,dunn2019_mnm,wisotsky2020_srv,lucaci2023_mhs,duchene2022_saturation}.

\ProjectName{} addresses this problem from a software/methods perspective. It does not replace codeml or HyPhy, and it does not claim that a simulation-trained classifier can by itself prove empirical positive selection. Instead, BABAPPA formalizes a guarded diagnostic workflow. It trains on explicit simulator branch-site truth, uses the user-supplied empirical MSA as the direct prediction input, uses conservative branch-site features, records leakage and context-shortcut risks, gates empirical inputs by applicability/OOD criteria, and reports diagnostic scores that must be interpreted with matched-null calibration and external reference tools. Alignment ensembles remain available as optional sensitivity analyses, not as a requirement for users who already provide a curated MSA.

This manuscript has three goals. First, it describes the scientific design of BABAPPA as a simulation-trained framework for branch-site support from a user-supplied codon MSA and tree. Second, it reports the completed 100K explicit branch-truth validation and the deployable model package produced from it. Third, it documents the present empirical bridge and WRKY pilot without overstating biological claims. The correct status is research-alpha: suitable for software/methods communication and reproducible evaluation, not for unsupported empirical discovery claims.

\section{Design principles}

\subsection{Simulator truth and empirical inference are separate}

BABAPPA distinguishes simulator truth from empirical data. During simulation validation, explicit branch-site labels are available from the generative process and are used as supervised targets. During empirical inference, no simulator truth exists and no truth-derived columns are allowed as inputs. The empirical bridge therefore includes a truth-column audit that rejects or flags columns such as \texttt{branch\_site\_truth}, \texttt{selected\_sites}, \texttt{truth}, \texttt{branch\_truth}, \texttt{oracle}, \texttt{y\_branch\_site}, \texttt{y\_site}, and \texttt{gene\_label}. This separation is central to the claim boundary of the project.

\subsection{The user-supplied MSA is the core empirical input}

The intended empirical interface is deliberately simple: the user supplies an aligned codon MSA and a treefile with matching tip labels, then selects a foreground tip, a comma-separated foreground set, or \texttt{all}. BABAPPA treats that MSA as the authoritative homology statement and does not realign it in the direct prediction command. This restores the primary software goal: from two biological inputs, report which branch-site rows receive simulation-trained episodic-selection support.

\subsection{Alignment ensembles are optional sensitivity analyses}

Alignment uncertainty remains scientifically important, but it is no longer required for the main user workflow. BABAPPA retains optional alignment-sensitivity workflows using \texttt{identity}, MAFFT, \texttt{BABAPPAlign}, and MUSCLE. PRANK and T-Coffee remain diagnostic-only. These workflows are useful when the user wants to test whether predictions are sensitive to alternative homology hypotheses, but curated user MSAs are scored directly.

\subsection{Conservative branch-site features are the deployable policy}

The deployable branch-site workflow uses \texttt{conservative\_branch\_site}. This feature policy was chosen after foreground-context ablation showed shortcut risk from context-only information. The policy prioritizes features that support branch-site scoring while reducing direct leakage from foreground-context identifiers and simulator truth. This does not remove all empirical uncertainty; it defines the safest current research-alpha feature surface.

\subsection{Applicability and abstention are first-class outputs}

For empirical data, BABAPPA estimates whether an input family is in-domain, borderline, or out-of-domain relative to the training envelope. It considers quantities such as taxon count, codon count, pairwise divergence, saturation proxy, gap and ambiguity burden, site-map mappability, feature missingness, and alignment disagreement. OOD cases are diagnostic-only and must not be interpreted as positive-selection calls.

\section{Validation progression}

BABAPPA progressed through increasingly explicit and conservative validation stages. Early gene-level experiments showed that direct gene-level classification was unstable under saturation and could compress probabilities. Site-level and branch-site formulations were then developed to localize sparse simulator truth before aggregating to higher levels. The explicit branch-truth pathway progressed from 1K to 10K and then to 100K, with Apple Silicon/MPS support added for the user's MacBook Pro M5 Max environment.

The completed 100K MPS validation is the current primary validation result. It used:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item truth mode: explicit simulator branch-site truth;
  \item feature policy: \texttt{conservative\_branch\_site};
  \item methods: \texttt{identity}, MAFFT, \texttt{BABAPPAlign}, and MUSCLE;
  \item saturation tiers: low, moderate, high, and extreme;
  \item 25,000 simulated families per tier;
  \item streamed/capped branch-site datasets;
  \item Apple Silicon/MPS neural execution with conservative memory safeguards.
\end{itemize}

The final 100K decision is a conditional pass. The qualification is practical rather than a failure of retained validation: raw and intermediate directories were pruned after completion to recover storage, so they cannot be directly revalidated in place. The retained summaries, truth audits, model artifacts, calibration outputs, aggregation outputs, controls, and stage markers support completion. This condition is recorded in the final validation report and should remain visible whenever the 100K result is cited.

\section{Empirical bridge}

The empirical bridge implements the following guarded workflow:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item user-supplied aligned codon MSA and tree validation;
  \item direct user-MSA site-map generation without realignment;
  \item branch-site feature extraction against the deployable feature schema;
  \item forbidden truth-column audit;
  \item applicability/OOD classification;
  \item deployable-model branch-site scoring;
  \item empirical report generation;
  \item optional alignment-sensitivity, codeml/HyPhy reference workflow preparation, parsing, and comparison;
  \item simulation-matched calibration planning and a conservative \texttt{run-simulation-matched-null-calibration} evidence-pack interface for dry-run validation and feature-level matched-null scoring.
\end{enumerate}

The direct command is \texttt{babappa predict-branch-sites}. It accepts \texttt{--msa}, \texttt{--tree}, \texttt{--foreground}, \texttt{--model-package}, \texttt{--outdir}, and \texttt{--device}. Its main outputs are \texttt{branch\_site\_predictions.tsv}, \texttt{branch\_predictions.tsv}, \texttt{gene\_summary.tsv}, \texttt{prediction\_report.md}, and \texttt{qc\_report.md}. This command is the intended end-user entry point.

This bridge is a software success, but it is not equivalent to biological discovery. It creates the infrastructure for responsible empirical analysis and makes the claim boundary explicit in every report. At present, the evidence-pack matched-null runner supports safe dry-run validation and conservative feature-level null scoring through the deployable model package. This is useful diagnostic calibration support, but it is not the same as a full raw sequence simulation plus alignment replay and should not be treated as a standalone empirical discovery criterion.

\section{Limitations}

BABAPPA has several explicit limitations.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The deployable model is simulation-trained. A simulator-to-real gap is unavoidable and must be tested.
  \item Empirical calibration is pending. BABAPPA diagnostic scores do not yet provide calibrated empirical $p$-values or final false-discovery guarantees.
  \item Empirical discovery claims are not finalized. A diagnostic-positive result is not, by itself, a publishable positive-selection claim.
  \item Alignment and taxon sampling remain important. Very high divergence, poor mappability, hidden paralogy, and taxon-mismatched trees can invalidate interpretation.
  \item External reference disagreement must be treated conservatively. BABAPPA-only signals should trigger matched-null calibration and controls, not discovery language.
  \item The context-only shortcut warning remains scientifically relevant. The conservative feature policy reduces but does not abolish the need for ablation and leakage audits.
  \item Raw/intermediate pruning limits direct reruns of some validators. The 100K validation is supported by retained summaries and artifacts, but the pruned raw directories cannot be revalidated in place.
  \item BABAPPA should be used alongside codeml, HyPhy, biological controls, and dataset-specific justification. It is not a replacement for reference workflows at this stage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

BABAPPA provides a validated research-alpha framework for guarded, simulation-trained branch-site positive-selection support and empirical diagnostic scoring from a user-supplied codon MSA and treefile. Its completed 100K explicit branch-truth MPS validation, deployable conservative model package, direct MSA/tree prediction interface, optional alignment-sensitivity workflow, empirical OOD gate, and WRKY diagnostic pilot make it ready for software/methods communication and reproducible evaluation. It is not ready for unsupported empirical positive-selection discovery claims. The next scientific step is to add close-taxa negative controls and interpret BABAPPA outputs jointly with matched-null calibration and codeml/HyPhy-style references before any empirical claim is made.
