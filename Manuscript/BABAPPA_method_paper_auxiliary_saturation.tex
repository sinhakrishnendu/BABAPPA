\begin{abstract}
Branch-site positive-selection analysis asks whether specific sites experienced episodic selection on specific phylogenetic branches. Standard likelihood tools such as codeml/PAML and HyPhy remain central references, but many practical analyses begin with a curated codon multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and a treefile and require a direct answer: which site on which branch is supported? \ProjectName{} (\ProjectExpansion{}) is a research-alpha, simulation-trained method designed for that interface. The core command accepts only a user-supplied aligned codon MSA, a matching tree, and a foreground definition (\texttt{all}, \texttt{leaves}, or specific tip labels). It treats the supplied MSA as the authoritative homology statement, performs no mandatory realignment, and reports branch-site scores, calls, aligned codon coordinates, and branch-specific de-gapped codon coordinates.

BABAPPA is intentionally standalone. It does not call codeml or HyPhy to obtain its inference, and it does not borrow their null models. Instead, it reports BABAPPA-native evidence: a deployable neural branch-site scorer trained on explicit simulator branch-site truth, an applicability/out-of-domain gate, and an empirical feature-null calibration layer that returns p-like BABAPPA quantities and support classes. codeml and HyPhy are used in this manuscript only as optional publication comparators, not as dependencies or ground truth.

The current implementation, \ProjectVersion{}, is based on a conservative explicit branch-truth Apple Silicon/MPS validation at 100,000 simulated families. The retained 100K validation is a conditional pass because raw and intermediate files were intentionally pruned after completion, but retained summaries, audits, model artifacts, calibrations, controls, and stage markers support the completed run. Branch-site neural AUROC across low, moderate, high, and extreme saturation tiers was 0.998605, 0.996962, 0.994527, and 0.989955. Branch-level AUROC was 0.999797, 0.999421, 0.998777, and 0.997873. Gene-level AUROC was 0.999697, 0.999616, 0.999508, and 0.999430. These values describe simulation-supervised validation, not empirical false-discovery rates.

A close-taxa WRKY pilot demonstrates the present empirical behavior. \texttt{WRKY\_candidate\_01} was rejected as an out-of-domain stress test with mean p-distance 0.725799. \texttt{WRKY\_candidate\_02\_close}, a seven-taxon close Brassicaceae WRKY33/AT2G38470 panel, was in-domain and BABAPPA diagnostic-positive, with max gene support 0.177189 and 6,954 called branch-site rows. codeml and HyPhy comparators were negative, while a 100-replicate feature-level BABAPPA matched-null calibration showed unusual called-row burden but not unusual maximum gene support. The result is therefore BABAPPA-only with mixed native-null support, not a cross-method biological discovery claim. The manuscript presents BABAPPA as a standalone complementary method whose outputs can be reported as BABAPPA-native calibrated branch-site support when QC, applicability, calibration, and biological context are documented.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Branch-site positive-selection inference is central to molecular evolution because adaptation may affect only a subset of sites on a subset of branches. Classical codon-model tools estimate shifts in nonsynonymous-to-synonymous rate ratio, $\omega=d_N/d_S$, and include PAML/codeml and HyPhy workflows such as aBSREL and MEME \citep{muse1994,goldman1994,yang2007,zhang2005,pond2011_bsrel,smith2015_absrel,murrell2012_meme,murrell2015_busted,pond2020_hyphy}. These likelihood approaches are mature and interpretable, but they are not the only possible evidence system. They also remain sensitive to alignment quality, taxon sampling, saturation, recombination, hidden paralogy, synonymous-rate variation, multinucleotide substitutions, and model misspecification \citep{anisimova2003_recombination,scheffler2006_recombination,jordan2012_alignment,redelings2014_erasing,venkat2018_mnm,dunn2019_mnm,wisotsky2020_srv,lucaci2023_mhs,duchene2022_saturation}.

BABAPPA was developed for a deliberately simple user-facing problem. A user has an orthologous codon MSA and a treefile. The user wants a program that asks for those two files, asks which foreground branches to evaluate, and reports which site on which branch is supported as episodic selection. The software should not require the user to track internal simulation files, alignment ensembles, raw tensors, or external likelihood tools before obtaining a BABAPPA result. The user-supplied MSA is treated as the biological homology hypothesis. If a user wants alignment-sensitivity analysis, BABAPPA can provide optional workflows, but the default prediction path does not realign the input.

This manuscript describes BABAPPA as a standalone complementary method. ``Standalone'' means that BABAPPA can produce its own evidence statement without codeml or HyPhy. ``Complementary'' means that BABAPPA does not claim likelihood-model equivalence and should be compared with classical tools in publication benchmarking. BABAPPA's evidence is simulation-trained and BABAPPA-native: it uses a deployable branch-site neural model, conservative feature policy, applicability/OOD checks, and empirical feature-null calibration. A manuscript may therefore report BABAPPA-native calibrated support, but should not call BABAPPA p-like values codeml/HyPhy likelihood-ratio p-values.

\section{Method Overview}

The direct BABAPPA workflow has five stages (Figure placeholder: direct workflow schematic).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Input}: aligned codon MSA FASTA and Newick treefile with matching labels.
  \item \textbf{Foreground selection}: all tips, leaves by default in the interactive interface, or user-specified tip labels.
  \item \textbf{Feature extraction}: conservative branch-site features are generated from the user MSA and tree context without simulator truth.
  \item \textbf{Scoring}: a deployable simulation-trained tier model scores each branch-site row.
  \item \textbf{BABAPPA-native calibration}: an empirical branch-shuffle feature-null backend estimates p-like support values for the observed BABAPPA score burden.
\end{enumerate}

The principal command is:

\begin{center}
\texttt{babappa predict-branch-sites --msa MSA --tree TREE --foreground all --null-replicates 1000}
\end{center}

The interactive command \texttt{babappa} prompts for only the MSA path, treefile path, and foreground mode. It then uses the default native-null replicate count. The explicit command allows users to set \texttt{--null-replicates 0} for a quick uncalibrated check, \texttt{100} for pilot calibration, and \texttt{1000+} for manuscript-strength BABAPPA-native calibration.

\section{Design Principles}

\subsection{The MSA supplied by the user is authoritative}

In direct prediction mode, BABAPPA does not realign the input. It assumes the user has supplied the MSA they want interpreted. This design keeps the tool aligned with the practical use case: a researcher already has a curated ortholog-family MSA and tree, and wants branch-site predictions. BABAPPA still validates that the MSA is equal-length, codon-compatible, and label-compatible with the tree.

\subsection{Simulator truth is never an empirical input}

Simulation truth is used only during training and validation. Empirical inference rejects or audits truth-derived columns such as \texttt{branch\_site\_truth}, \texttt{selected\_sites}, \texttt{truth}, \texttt{branch\_truth}, \texttt{oracle}, \texttt{y\_branch\_site}, \texttt{y\_site}, \texttt{gene\_label}, \texttt{positive\_label}, and \texttt{simulated\_label}. This prevents the simulation-supervised training target from leaking into empirical scoring.

\subsection{The feature policy is conservative}

The deployable branch-site model uses the \texttt{conservative\_branch\_site} feature policy. This choice followed foreground-context ablation analyses showing shortcut risk from context-only features. The conservative policy is intended to preserve branch-site signal while reducing direct context leakage. It does not remove all model limitations, but it defines the safest current feature surface for research-alpha deployment.

\subsection{Applicability and abstention are explicit outputs}

BABAPPA evaluates whether a family is \texttt{in\_domain}, \texttt{borderline}, or \texttt{out\_of\_domain}. The gate considers taxon count, codon count, pairwise divergence, saturation proxy, gap burden, ambiguity burden, site-map mappability, feature missingness, and available training-envelope information. Out-of-domain cases are marked diagnostic-only and should be treated as stress tests rather than biological calls.

\subsection{codeml and HyPhy are publication comparators, not dependencies}

BABAPPA includes a separate \texttt{publication\_benchmark/} pipeline for manuscript evaluation against codeml and HyPhy. That pipeline is not part of the core user-facing BABAPPA inference path. It exists to generate comparison tables for peer review, investigate failure modes, and contextualize BABAPPA-native evidence.

\section{Training and Validation}

BABAPPA was trained and validated using explicit simulator branch-site truth. The final retained validation state is a conservative 100K Apple Silicon/MPS run with:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item 100,000 simulated families total;
  \item four saturation tiers: low, moderate, high, extreme;
  \item 25,000 families per tier;
  \item explicit branch-site truth;
  \item \texttt{conservative\_branch\_site} feature policy;
  \item production aligners: identity, MAFFT \citep{katoh2013_mafft}, BABAPPAlign, and MUSCLE \citep{edgar2004_muscle};
  \item streamed/capped branch-site datasets;
  \item MPS-compatible training and scoring infrastructure.
\end{itemize}

The validation decision is a conditional pass. The condition is due to storage management: raw and intermediate simulation/alignment/tensor directories were intentionally pruned after completion. Retained summaries, truth audits, model artifacts, calibrations, aggregation outputs, controls, and stage markers support the completed validation state. This condition should be reported whenever the 100K validation is cited.

\section{Direct Prediction Outputs}

The direct command writes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{branch\_site\_predictions.tsv}: one row per scored branch-site combination;
  \item \texttt{branch\_predictions.tsv}: branch-level support summary;
  \item \texttt{gene\_summary.tsv}: gene-level BABAPPA summary;
  \item \texttt{prediction\_report.md}: human-readable interpretation;
  \item \texttt{qc\_report.md}: input and applicability summary;
  \item \texttt{babappa\_native\_null/}: native-null distributions and observed-versus-null summaries when \texttt{--null-replicates > 0}.
\end{itemize}

Branch-site prediction rows include both alignment coordinates and de-gapped coordinates. The key fields are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{msa\_codon\_site}: one-based codon column in the supplied MSA;
  \item \texttt{aligned\_codon\_site}: equivalent aligned codon coordinate;
  \item \texttt{branch\_degapped\_codon\_site}: one-based codon coordinate in the scored branch after removing gapped codons;
  \item \texttt{branch\_codon}: codon carried by the scored branch at the aligned site.
\end{itemize}

For manuscript reporting, the most important gene-level fields are \texttt{result\_class}, \texttt{babappa\_native\_result\_class}, \texttt{babappa\_native\_evidence\_class}, \texttt{babappa\_native\_null\_replicates}, \texttt{p\_babappa\_called\_rows}, \texttt{p\_babappa\_max\_gene\_support}, \texttt{p\_babappa\_max\_branch\_support}, \texttt{applicability\_status}, and \texttt{tier\_model}.

\section{BABAPPA-Native Null Calibration}

BABAPPA's standalone evidence layer is an empirical feature-null backend. For each replicate, branch- and foreground-specific feature columns are shuffled across the empirical feature table while preserving the overall feature surface and family size. The shuffled replicate is scored through the same deployable model and threshold policy as the observed family. BABAPPA then compares observed metrics with the null distribution:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item maximum gene support;
  \item maximum branch support;
  \item number of called positive branch-site rows;
  \item maximum site score;
  \item high-score site-score quantiles.
\end{itemize}

The p-like right-tail value is computed as
\[
p_{\mathrm{BABAPPA}} = \frac{1 + \#\{x_{\mathrm{null}} \ge x_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{N_{\mathrm{null}} + 1}.
\]

BABAPPA classifies native-null evidence as \texttt{strong\_babappa\_native\_support}, \texttt{babappa\_native\_support}, \texttt{not\_significant\_under\_babappa\_native\_null}, or \texttt{underpowered\_native\_null}. The combined result class is stored as \texttt{babappa\_native\_result\_class}. These values are BABAPPA-native and should be reported as such. They are not codeml/HyPhy likelihood-ratio p-values.

\section{Publication Benchmarking Against codeml and HyPhy}

For publication, BABAPPA includes a separate benchmark harness under \texttt{publication\_benchmark/}. This harness runs BABAPPA-native calibrated prediction, prepares codeml and HyPhy reference folders, optionally runs those tools, parses outputs, and writes comparison tables. It is not used by BABAPPA's normal inference command.

The benchmark is intended for curated manuscript panels with known or literature-supported positives, conserved negatives, alignment-sensitive families, saturated families, short/low-information families, and paralogy-risk families. codeml and HyPhy are interpreted as external comparators. They are not assumed to be ground truth, and BABAPPA is not tuned to mimic them.

\section{Limitations}

BABAPPA is research-alpha and has explicit limitations.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The deployable model is simulation-trained, so simulator-to-real transfer must be studied empirically.
  \item BABAPPA-native p-like values are not likelihood-ratio p-values and should not be described as codeml/HyPhy equivalents.
  \item A raw diagnostic-positive score without native-null calibration is not sufficient for manuscript interpretation.
  \item OOD families, high saturation, hidden paralogy, tree/MSA mismatch, or low mappability can invalidate biological interpretation.
  \item The 100K validation is a conditional pass because raw intermediates were pruned after completion.
  \item External disagreement should be interpreted as method contrast, not automatic truth or failure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

BABAPPA now matches the original software goal: from a user-supplied codon MSA and treefile, it reports branch-site support, branch-level summaries, gene-level summaries, de-gapped site coordinates, QC/applicability status, and BABAPPA-native calibrated evidence. It is a standalone complementary method, not a wrapper around codeml or HyPhy. codeml and HyPhy remain valuable external comparators for publication benchmarking, but BABAPPA's own inference is based on its simulation-trained deployable model and native empirical feature-null calibration.
